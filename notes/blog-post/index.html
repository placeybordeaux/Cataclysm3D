What differs from Cataclysm (254 entries)

Every material difference we could document, in 14 groups. Each entry says what Cataclysm does, what CDDA-3D does, why, and where it's recorded. Filter by reason:

1. Rendering and presentation30
  1. Camera. Cataclysm is a top-down grid of ASCII glyphs or tiles. CDDA-3D is first person by default. Over-the-shoulder and a top-down "classic" camera are one key (V) away.
    medium adaptationARCHITECTURE.md "Visual style"; DESIGN_V2 decision 5
  2. Art direction. Cataclysm draws 2D sprites or glyphs. CDDA-3D renders "Project Shadowglass" 3D pixel art: a low-resolution target upscaled with nearest filtering, point-filtered textures, banded lighting, a palette/LUT grade, ordered dither and outlines.
    medium adaptationARCHITECTURE.md; docs/LOOKS.md
  3. Texel density taken from the tileset. UltiCa draws a 1 m tile as 32 px. CDDA-3D paints every 3D surface at 32 texels per metre, so the world is literally built from Cataclysm's pixel art.
    medium adaptationdocs/FURNITURE.md, CHARACTERS.md
  4. Five switchable "looks": classic, crisp, voxel, terrain and full. The user picked voxel as the default in the style gallery, along with a 168 m draw distance and sprite first-person hands (7c2620c).
    medium adaptationdocs/LOOKS.md; commit 7c2620c
  5. Monsters and NPCs are low-poly 3D rigs, not sprites. Each body is procedurally built from boxes, painted from its UltiCa sprite, and animated at a stepped 10 fps. It turns to face where it walks or what it fights. The camera-facing billboards are still an option.
    medium adaptationdocs/CHARACTERS.md; ?chars=billboard
  6. Body plans from BN data. There are more than 10 archetypes (humanoid, quadruped, insect, flyer, blob, serpent, plant, machine…). They are chosen from BN bodytype, then species, flags and symbol, and sized from volume in ml. Zombie builds (fat, brute, hulk, skeleton, child, armoured) come from the monster id. Cataclysm itself has no 3D shape data at all.
    medium adaptationdocs/CHARACTERS.md
  7. Individual zombies get quirks. Each zombie's seed gives it a hunch, a head tilt, a limp or a dragging arm, plus a shambling lurch. None of this exists in Cataclysm.
    medium adaptationCHARACTERS.md round 2/3
  8. Procedural NPC looks. NPCs get six builds, seven skin tones, eight hair colours, several hair styles and headgear, all stable per uid. NPCs are also painted from their worn-gear composite, so a red jacket reads as a red jacket in 3D.
    medium adaptationCHARACTERS.md round 6
  9. Furniture is modelled. In Cataclysm, furniture is a sprite on a tile. CDDA-3D has 75 low-poly archetypes: neighbours join up (long tables drop their inner legs, counter runs turn corners), pieces back onto walls, and chairs face tables.
    medium adaptationdocs/FURNITURE.md
  10. Walls and floors are painted at real scale. Bricks are 25×8 cm, planks 12.5 cm and linoleum tiles 25 cm. Interior faces get drywall or wallpaper chosen per building, rooms get baseboards and crown moulding, and roofs get shingles. Cataclysm has one sprite per terrain.
    medium adaptationFURNITURE.md "Surfaces"
  11. Render-only set dressing. Houses get pictures, calendars, grime, water stains, peeling paint, bloody handprints, rugs, unlit ceiling lamps, house numbers, porch lamps, gutters and graffiti. All of it is deterministic per room or building, and none of it is a game object.
    medium adaptationFURNITURE.md round 3
  12. "Wrecked" houses. The building seed picks kept, dirty or wrecked. Wrecked houses get toppled chairs, bookcases on their faces, floor clutter and junk heaps. This is visual only; BN's map items are unchanged.
    medium adaptationFURNITURE.md round 4
  13. Ground items are drawn as 16 generic shapes (box, bottle, can, gun, blanket…). Each is sized from the item's volume, with small items scaled up so they read. At most three are drawn per tile, resting on the furniture's real top surface.
    medium adaptationFURNITURE.md
  14. Trees are modelled species families: round, willow, conifer, birch, fruit, sapling and dead. Colours come from the seasonal sprite, so trees blossom in spring, fruit in summer and get snow caps in winter. They have 3 LOD rings with no pop to a billboard.
    medium adaptationdocs/FLORA.md
  15. Distance is drawn. Cataclysm shows only what is in sight. CDDA-3D fades distance toward the sky colour and adds a far ground ring and an overmap skyline out to the horizon.
    medium adaptationdocs/READABILITY.md
  16. Dormant monsters are drawn. Beyond the 60-tile reality bubble, monsters are not simulated, as in Cataclysm, but they are still drawn as idle far figures up to about 130 m. A horde down a long road is visible.
    medium adaptationCHARACTERS.md round 2; monai.ts ACTIVE_RADIUS = 60
  17. Far readability aids. Limbs get up to 45 % thicker between 22 and 55 m, and hostiles get a small self-lit emphasis from 16 to 45 m, so they never sink into dusk.
    new-player designCHARACTERS.md
  18. Weather shows in the sky. BN's light_modifier and sight_penalty drive overcast, a dimmed sun and closer haze. Wipers sweep while it rains.
    medium adaptationREADABILITY.md; VEHICLES.md round 4
  19. Toon form lighting. Light comes in 4 bands with a world-fixed side fill, so a house seen from its shadow side still shows its corner. Lamp pools are dithered instead of drawn as rings.
    medium adaptationREADABILITY.md
  20. Night uses Cataclysm's light units in a light map. Flashlights, fires and headlights are dynamic lights.
    medium adaptationARCHITECTURE.md; vision.ts
  21. Fire is animated. UltiCa's flame frames play on three crossed, world-fixed cards, with rising ember meshes.
    medium adaptationCHARACTERS.md round 6
  22. Hit feedback you can see. There is hit-stop (45–85 ms), flinch and shove, and bodies stagger, topple and bounce with a thud. Blood and dust fly, and death falls along the killing blow. Cataclysm reports all of this in text.
    medium adaptationdocs/COMBAT_FEEL.md
  23. Arrows are objects. They are visible meshes in flight and stuck in bodies, walls and the ground, and they can be picked up again.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Archery", CHARACTERS.md round 5
  24. Mannequins and statues are posed humanoid rigs baked into the chunk.
    medium adaptationCHARACTERS.md round 5
  25. Broken windows and doors are drawn as openings. BN drops their WINDOW/DOOR flags, so a naive renderer showed a floor decal of glass or a floating pane. CDDA-3D draws jagged shards in the frame. (render)
    fidelity fixFURNITURE.md
  26. Stairs are modelled as flights, spirals and ladders with stairwells cut into the floor above. Before, stairs were a block of steps with a hole.
    medium adaptationdocs/STAIRS.md
  27. Vehicles are one modelled body each, not a grid of part tiles (see §6).
    medium adaptationdocs/VEHICLES.md
  28. 3D first-person hands and held weapons. Arms are painted from your outfit, and there are per-weapon variants (revolver, M1911, AR, AK, pump or break-action…). Bows stay 2D art while drawn, because that read better. The user's defaults switched the hands back to sprites (7c2620c).
    medium adaptationCHARACTERS.md rounds 3–5
  29. Style gallery debug page. /?gallery compares every visual switch on fixed scenes, live, as A/B pairs or as a matrix. The user chose the shipped defaults with it. (tooling)
    new-player designdocs/GALLERY.md
  30. Title logo and big buttons. Cataclysm's title is a terminal row. CDDA-3D has a pixel-art logo and a big-button menu. The ASCII logo and the one-line row are still options.
    new-player designADAPTATION.md #3; QA_V2 #21
2. Controls and real time19
  1. Turns become seconds. One BN turn (100 moves) takes 1/3 real second (ACTION_SCALE = 3), so a speed-100 walker covers 3 tiles/s.
    medium adaptationsrc/core/time.ts
  2. Melee runs on a slower clock (MELEE_SCALE = 2 turns/s), "so fights read blow by blow". At 3 turns/s, a pack of zombies shredded you before you could react.
    medium adaptationsrc/core/time.ts
  3. A game day is about one real hour (CLOCK_SCALE = 24). Day length is a Custom difficulty knob.
    medium adaptationdocs/BALANCE.md "Real-time pacing"
  4. Long actions warp the clock. A night's sleep takes about 16 s (2 real s per game hour), and crafting, reading and waiting take 2–8 real s. Like Cataclysm activities, they stop when a hostile comes into view.
    medium adaptationBALANCE.md; ARCHITECTURE.md
  5. Tactical pause (` `` or Pause/Break). A turn-based game never needs one. Here it freezes the simulation, and you can still look around and open every window.
    medium adaptationBALANCE.md "Tactical pause"
  6. Menus pause time on Accessible. On Classic, time runs in the inventory and crafting screens, while the Esc menu always pauses. BN menus take no time at all, but real time can't be stopped per keypress.
    medium adaptationBALANCE.md
  7. WASD and mouse-look, with CDDA letter keys as accelerators. Where the two collide, CDDA's keys moved. For example, F is the flashlight and fire mode moved to b; ^ still takes the controls.
    medium adaptationARCHITECTURE.md "Game design conversion rules"; STATUS.md controls
  8. Every key is rebindable, and layouts are handled. AZERTY is learned from the layout map, CapsLock no longer turns e into E (eat), and AltGr and non-Latin layouts work.
    new-player designdocs/UX_REVIEW.md #1–4, #10
  9. e does the obvious thing at the crosshair. o opens a world context menu, and in menus a right-click opens a context menu.
    new-player designdocs/UI2.md
  10. Hitting things smashes them. Swinging at a window, door, fence or piece of furniture with nothing alive in front of you smashes it. In Cataclysm you need the smash key (X still works).
    new-player designcommit c2c0095; lead's live-fix report 2026-09-30
  11. Movement slides round a lone blocked corner (a tree tile). Axis-by-axis sliding had nothing to slide along.
    medium adaptationADAPTATION.md #33; tests/physics_corner.test.ts
  12. Side-step: Space, or a double-tap of A, D or S.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  13. Timed actions show a ring at the crosshair that says "stand still". Moving interrupts, and the reason flashes at the crosshair instead of appearing in the log.
    new-player designADAPTATION.md #4
  14. A movement key already held when an action starts is ignored, both for cancelling and for walking. The user's "I stopped lighting a fire" report was an activity cancelled on the same tick.
    medium adaptationlead, session 1, 2026-09-30 05:32
  15. Mouse capture rules. Esc resumes with any key, F11 gives fullscreen with keyboard lock, and a "Leave site?" guard catches Ctrl+W. These are browser pointer-lock problems a terminal game never has.
    engine limitationdocs/UX_REVIEW.md
  16. Wait menu in seconds and minutes ("20 seconds", not "20 heartbeats").
    new-player designADAPTATION.md #7
  17. Stairs are walked. In Cataclysm, < and > move you a level. In CDDA-3D you walk up the flight, and < and > climb the nearest flight for you (see §7).
    medium adaptationdocs/STAIRS.md
  18. Driving uses a direct throttle by default. BN's 4-mph cruise steps are an option (see §6).
    medium adaptationARCHITECTURE.md "Vehicles"
  19. Look mode (x) uses the mouse.
    medium adaptationUX_REVIEW.md #22
3. Combat and aim28
  1. Free aim is the default. The shot goes where the reticle was when you clicked, and there are no to-hit dice for the survivor ("Steady hands"). BN's dice are the dice Aim model option.
    new-player designdocs/COMBAT_FEEL.md "Aim models"; user feedback 2026-09-30
  2. Skill is the reticle's weave. Its amplitude is BN's mean lateral shot error (weapon, dex, hands and skill dispersion). Skill 0 with a pistol is about 1.8°, skill 5 about 0.5°. Low skill also weaves faster.
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  3. Skill also scales damage (×0.75 at skill 0 up to ×1.3 at 10), crit chance, stagger and recoil recovery.
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  4. Hit zones come from the drawn 3D model. A head is ×2 (BN's head severity cap), legs and limbs ×0.75, and a graze on the outer 20 % of the body ×0.5. A ray between the legs misses.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md; render/aimPick.ts
  5. Recoil is a kick, not a spread. BN's recoil (MOA) kicks the aim up, and it recovers faster with skill.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  6. Optional timing minigame. Hold breath for a NOW window, loose the bow as a ring closes, active reload, and "melee on the beat" (×1.25).
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Timing model extras"
  7. Melee swings have phases: wind-up, strike, contact and recovery. They add up to BN's attack_speed, and the blow resolves at contact against what the swept arc finds then. In v1 it resolved on the click, before the swing played.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Diagnosis" #1, "What changed"
  8. A failed melee roll becomes a glancing blow in free aim (×0.5, no technique, no crit), not a clean miss.
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  9. Melee zones. A head hit does ×1.5 and may stun, and a leg hit may knock the target down. The target is the zombie under the crosshair, found with a fan of rays against the model boxes.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md round 3
  10. Monsters telegraph their blows. Each blow winds up for 0.4–0.8 s on Accessible (0.25–0.6 s on Classic): the monster rears back, flashes warm and then white-hot, and a snarl plays. Stepping out of reach makes it whiff. BN has no wind-ups.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Monster telegraphs"
  11. Guard (hold RMB) maps onto BN's block_hit. While guarding, the block attempt always happens (+8 score), and a well-timed raise is a parry (+10) that stuns the attacker for 0.8 s.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Active defence"
  12. Side-step dodge. Space moves you 1.3 m and gives +6 dodge against blows in the next 0.3 s. Normally the step simply leaves the lunge's reach.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  13. Grabs are a mash-e struggle bar. Each press rolls BN's break-free check. Before, walking away was the only way out.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  14. A staggering blow breaks a grab on Accessible (20 % of HP or a crit).
    new-player designBALANCE.md "First fight"; FEEL.staggerBreaksGrab
  15. Dodges are visible. A monster BN would let dodge hops back and aside before contact. Plain zombies (dodge 0) never do.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  16. Every miss says why at the crosshair: "dodged", "clumsy swing", "blocked by cover", "fell short: out of range (22 m)", "wide: archery skill too low (0)"…
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md; QA_V2
  17. Monsters take slots around you within 3.5 m instead of queueing in single file. They stop 0.2 m inside their reach, with hysteresis.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Spacing"
  18. Enemy fire is real rounds in the air. Bullets fly at 90 m/s and are drawn as streaks; arrows and stones fly visibly. Walls stop them, bystanders get hit, and near misses make a zip and a flinch. BN's dispersion still sets the direction.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md round 3; 08120ee
  19. Bows are drawn and loosed. RMB draws for BN's draw time and LMB looses. Arrows follow a gravity arc and take time to arrive. BN's strength bands and recovery odds (1 in 25 wooden arrows break) apply.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md "Archery"
  20. Only powder weapons flash (firearms and launchers). Before this fix a longbow "sounds like a gun and has the muzzle flash" (the user).
    fidelity fixCOMBAT_FEEL.md; QA_V2
  21. ADS zoom (70° to 50°) with RMB on a gun, and reloads take visible time (0.7–2.5 s from BN's reload cost).
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  22. Accessible slows monster swings so each cycle is at least the tell plus 0.35 s: about 1.4 → 1.05 attacks/s for a zombie. Classic keeps BN's swing rate exactly (tested).
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  23. Tracers and bullet marks, which stay on walls for 30 s.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  24. First-fight hints by the crosshair: "Your aim sways — fire as it crosses", "Aim for the head — ×2", "Hold right mouse: guard · Space: side-step".
    new-player designCOMBAT_FEEL.md round 3
  25. Throwing (G) goes where you look.
    medium adaptationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  26. NPCs and monsters still use BN's dice for their own attacks.
    simplificationCOMBAT_FEEL.md
  27. Melee has no sway. In free aim, a swing's timing is the skill.
    simplificationCOMBAT_FEEL.md known gaps
  28. Ammo stretches further. Well-aimed shots don't miss, so a skill-0 pistol needs about 3 rounds per kill instead of about 5, while ammo spawns are unchanged. This is flagged as an open decision for the user.

1. Fidelity bugs: when the port disagreed with Cataclysm

These are the best blog material: each is a case where reading Cataclysm's JSON "the obvious way" was wrong, and the fix came from reading the C++.

The weather was always a snowstorm

"The biggest finding is a real bug: with BN data the weather was always a snowstorm. BN's weather_type entries keep their thresholds in a requirements object. weatherConditions only read DDA's condition and required_weathers, so every weather type matched and the last one, snowstorm, won on every refresh. That explains the "Snowstorm -7 °C on a green spring morning". It also meant the whole game ran in snowstorm col[d]…"

— a subagent (new-player adaptation pass), Sep 29 23:45, session 2. A leftover from the DDA→BN switch: DDA and BN spell the same concept differently, and a matcher that finds no conditions matches everything.

Flashlights: first 1000× too hungry, then dark

"Cause: Bright Nights gives item power draw in milliwatts, but our code read it as watts, so everything electric drained 1000× too fast. Your flashlight (10 W) was emptying a full battery in about 10 game seconds, well under one real second."

— the lead, Sep 29 18:08, session 1, answering the user's "flashlights are running out of power almost immediately". Just before, while fixing it:

"The existing test was written around the bug: its comment literally says "10 kW power_draw: 1 kJ per 0.1 s". I'll correct it to BN's real rate."

— the lead, Sep 29 18:08. And the next night, a second, unrelated flashlight bug:

"Flashlights gave no light at all. Bright Nights stores item light under a different field than the one the code read. So a flashlight lit nothing, and F said "You don't have a light source" while you carried a phone with a flashlight app."

— a subagent (new-player adaptation pass), Sep 30 00:26, session 2.

1.4-metre tyres

"Wheel sizes were a bug I found: BN gives wheel sizes in cm, but they were read as inches, so tyres were 1.4 m tall and poked through the bonnet into the view."

— a subagent (driving camera & visibility), Sep 29 11:09, session 1.

These two unit bugs became a standing rule in docs/STATUS.md: "Units come from BN's C++, not guesses. Two big wave-2 bugs were unit mistakes: power_draw is mW (flashlights died in a second), wheel sizes are cm (1.4 m tyres)." The wave-3 vehicle agent then reported "Wheel size comes from BN's rim diameter in cm (checked against the C++ loader)."

Broken windows drawn as floor decals

"Broken windows and doors were really mis-classified. CDDA drops their WINDOW/DOOR flags, so they were drawn as a floor decal or a fence pane. That's where the floating blue squares came from."

— a subagent (furniture and props models), Sep 30 00:38, session 2.

Nothing could be dug, anywhere

"Nothing could be built on grass or dirt. BN decides whether ground can be dug from the terrain's digging_results; our code checked DDA's DIGGABLE flag, which only 2 BN terrains still have. So barricades, pits and graves were "too hard to dig" everywhere."

— a subagent (new-player adaptation pass), Sep 30 02:19, session 2.

Fires that went out with the wood unburnt

"Fires going out with the wood unburnt (BN fidelity, src/game/fields.ts).
