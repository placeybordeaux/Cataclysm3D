Building Cataclysm3D

One prompt, four multiple-choice answers and 5 h 26 min later, a lead Claude Code session and 24 subagents had a playable real-time, first-person Cataclysm in the browser. Four weeks and 148 subagent runs later it is 168k lines of TypeScript, with Bright Nights' sewers, subways and labs down to z −10. This page collects the numbers, the story, the pictures and the quotes for a long-form post.

The original prompt

the user · Sep 28, 22:25 PDT · verbatimYou have a large task ahead of you. You're going to be making a real time 3d version of Cataclysm Dark Days Ahead. You'll be aiming to keep as much of the style, content & approaches to game design as possible while converting this to a fully real time 3d game. This is an excersize in determining how much you can do *automomously* there will be a brief period at the start to ask the user questions. After that you are expected to lead a team of subagents to deliver a fully working game. You'll be judged on completeness, how much you were able to retain and enjoyment. Bonus points for closely using similar graphical style by immitating the graphic appraoch used by project shadowglass. Now is your opportunity for asking questions go!

Since the first snapshot

The first version of this page was written on Sep 30, a day and a half in. The project kept going for four more weeks at a slower, more conversational pace: the user played a long game and reported what felt wrong, and the lead turned each report into a subagent. TypeScript grew from 111k to 168k lines and tests from 1,238 to 2,325. These images are the agents' own evidence screenshots, not renders made for the blog.

Recreating the official screenshots

Two published images, rebuilt tile by tile in the engine: the Cataclysm: Bright Nights readme title image and the Cataclysm: DDA “UltiCa showcase, Sep 2021”. Each panel was transcribed by eye into a tile grid (terrain, furniture, monsters, items, fire, blood, electricity), stamped into an empty field and photographed twice. From straight above it matches the original's framing; from the ground it shows what the same place looks like in first person.

Original on the left or top, ours on the right or bottom. Default voxel look throughout. What matched and what couldn't be recreated is in recreations.md. For example, the temple panel comes from the Arcana mod, which we don't load, so it's rebuilt from core stone, pews and a red carpet.

Suggested post outline

  1. The bet. One paragraph prompt, “how much can you do autonomously”. Show the prompt and the four questions.
  2. The first night (5h 26m). Lead writes a scaffold and shared architecture doc, then fans out waves of 5–6 agents in git worktrees and merges them. Evac shelter boots at 45 min. Commits-per-hour and LOC growth chart.
  3. The morning after. The user finds two bugs in minutes that three QA agents missed, because QA drove the game through debug APIs. Birth of the “real input only” rule.
  4. Changing the brief. From faithful DDA port to Bright Nights for a new audience; modern UI; the tutorial question the user answered with “achievements and dynamic unlocks”.
  5. When the data lies. Snowstorm weather, milliwatts, centimetre wheels, the DIGGABLE flag, broken windows as floor decals, fires that fast-forward badly. The “units from source” rule.
  6. A TV adaptation of a book. Wave 3: what Shadowglass actually does, sprites as inspiration for 3D models, aim instead of dice, cars that aren't boxes. Before/after images and the style gallery.
  7. What it cost. Tokens by phase, cache reads vs output, lead vs subagents, the usage-limit pause, and what the numbers don't include.
  8. What's different, and what's missing. Pick 10 from the differences list; link the full list.
  9. Working this way. Parallel worktrees with a merging lead, variants behind switches instead of guessing, a live bridge into the player's tab, honest “best guess” fixes.
  10. The long tail. Four weeks of play-and-report: a tank with no turret instructions, a fart-like zombie snarl, buildings popping in on the horizon. Gap packages handed between sessions through a doc; one 41k-line squash merge.
  11. What's next. Merging VR and the QoL branches, vehicles v3 beyond phase 1 (toward a deathmobile), walking around inside big vehicles, the uncommitted vehicle judder fix.

Method and caveats

  • Transcripts: 20 lead sessions and 148 subagent JSONL files, streamed into SQLite (scripts/load_transcripts.py) and analysed separately (scripts/analyse.py). Usage is de-duplicated per message id, taking the maximum of each field.
  • Claude Code's own meter for session 1 shows more than the transcripts (8.88M output vs 3.18M; 4.66B vs 3.73B cache reads), most likely calls that never appear as messages, such as the auto-mode classifier. Treat transcript totals as a lower bound. The first gap-work session (Oct 4–5) left commits but no transcript here, so that phase is undercounted.
